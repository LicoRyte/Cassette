# Cassie

To The Web!: https://licoryte.github.io/Cassie/

A focus timer like a vintage tape deck: one big clock in a console frame, made to be satisfying to watch while it runs,
on a desk you can move across for your own notes, photos and tables. No rewards, no streaks to protect. Just the time
passing nicely.

It works offline: open it once and it saves everything it needs. In Chrome or Edge (computer or Android) use
**Install app** (or the browser menu) to get it as its own app with an icon. On iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen.
Everything is saved in this browser.

## Controls

Press **?** (or the ? key at the top left, next to Settings) for every gesture. Cassie keeps only a few keys, the ones you'd
expect: `Space` starts or pauses, `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` (or `Ctrl+Y`) undo and redo, `Delete` removes what's picked, `Ctrl+A`
picks everything, `Ctrl+D` duplicates what's picked, `Ctrl+V` pastes (pictures too), `Esc` puts down the note you're writing or closes things, `Enter` starts writing
in a picked card, and `Shift+Enter` while writing starts a new note tied to it. Notes take the usual text keys (`Ctrl+B`, `Ctrl+I`,
`Ctrl+U`...) and tables move between cells with `Tab`, `Enter` and the arrows. Everything else is a click: the player's keys, the
buttons along the top, the left column, and a right-click on a note or a table cell.

## What's in it

- **The two top corners:** the top left holds the app and the timer as plain words, no frames: **Settings** and **?**, then **Tapes**, then **Mini** and **Full** side by side (the open one is underlined). The top right is only for being online: the **Room** key (Join a room, Room · waiting, or Room with a mint dot once friends are here), the faces of who's here, the message line, and your own face, which opens the room panel just below. Message bubbles stay under the line at the top right: the last twenty, yours and your friends', each with the time, the newest on top; scroll the column to read back. They're cleared when you leave the room. If the Mini window is open, a friend's message also shows across its top for a few seconds, so you see it while you work in another app. With two or more friends, click a face to write only to them; click it again for everyone. Nothing on the screen fades while you focus: the keys and the desk stay as they are.
- **The front page:** Cassie opens on a front page: the name, and your workspaces. A workspace is a whole desk of its own: its boards, notes, tables, photos and strings (the timer, settings, tapes and room are the same in all of them). **Continue** (or Enter) goes back into the one you were in: its name, how many boards and notes, and when you last saved a backup (in red when it's been over two weeks, or never). **Other workspaces** are listed under it, the last opened first: click one to open it (the page fades and Cassie starts again in it). Pointing at a row shows its keys: rename it right in the row, save a backup of it, or delete it (click twice; the one you're in can't be deleted, so open another first). With more than six, a box finds one by name. **New workspace** starts an empty desk with a name you give it, and **Load a backup** makes a backup file a workspace of its own; neither touches the others. Under them, Save a backup and Rename are for the one you were in, and **Save a backup of everything** (with more than one workspace) saves every workspace in one file; loading that file with Load a backup brings every workspace in it back, next to yours. The house key at the top of the left column (above Boards) brings it back; Esc steps back.
- **How it works:** the ? key at the top left opens a card with every key and gesture. A column of icon keys down the left side is for making things: the Front page (your workspaces), the Boards menu, Note, Photo, Table, Hopper, Find, Undo and Redo. The bar along the bottom only shows while something is picked, with Group, Ungroup and Delete for several things. A note's and a table's own tools open on a right-click (while the mouse is over a new note or table, faint "Right-click for tools" words float just above it and follow it, until you right-click that one; on a touch screen, hold a finger on a note or a cell for half a second instead). Tapes, Mini and Full sit at the top left beside the ? key. All are hollow keys like the timer's. Point at any of these keys (the left column, the bottom bar, the two top corners) and a small card shows at once beside it: the key's name in the key colour, its shortcut as a little key, and a line on what it does (a typing shortcut, like # then space, shows as "or type"; long names wrap inside the card), with a small arrow pointing at the key.
- **Tapes** (T, or Tapes at the top left): the cassette opens as a panel beside the left column, framed like Room (a thin frame with the key colour along the top), with your music and the mixtape side by side (it stays put; the desk moves freely behind it), and **Save cassette** puts it away (everything in it is kept as you go). It has two panels side by side. On the left, **your music**: every audio file you've added (drop files on it, or + Add), kept in this browser. On the right, the **mixtape**: music on or off and the volume, which mixtape is in the deck (new, rename, delete), and its two sides: Side A plays while you focus, Side B on breaks. Drag tracks from your music onto a side (or use +A / +B), drag them to reorder, between sides, or off. Music plays only while the timer runs, fades in and out, crossfades from one track into the next, and dips while the chime rings. When focus turns into a break or back, the deck flips over with a clunk and the music changes sides; the line under the counter shows the side and what's playing.
- **The deck:** real tape: the reels hold brown tape. The accent colour (keys, reels, outlines) is the desk's own, on the light desk and the dark one.
- **The feel of the machine:** music speeds up from a slow, low pitch when you press Play and wind down when you pause or reach 00:00, like a real deck's motor. Each key sounds like itself: Play latches with a firm click, Pause pops back up, Rewind whirrs. 
- **The desk's light:** notes and photos are lit on the side facing the clock, more the closer they are, in the clock's colour (cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on breaks, dim when paused). Paper has a faint fibre grain and a lifted corner, and the grain and a far layer of dots drift a little as you move the mouse.
- **Views:** **Mini** (or M) opens a mini deck, a small window that stays on top of your other apps with the time, the side, the reels, a progress bar and the keys, so the timer is there while you study in something else (Chrome and Edge). **Full** (or F) leaves only the clock, big, with nothing else on the screen; the bar comes back while the mouse moves and the pointer hides when it's still. F or Esc comes back.
- **Mixtapes:** the cassette's Music tab keeps **your music**, every audio file you add, stored in this browser (with each track's length), and **mixtapes** made from it: named tapes, each with a Side A (focus) and a Side B (breaks). Pick which mixtape is in the deck; make new ones, rename or delete them. Drag tracks to put a side in order, to move them from side to side, from your music onto a side, or off a side (the file stays in your music); +A and +B add a track in one click; double-click a track's name to rename it right there (Enter keeps it, Esc doesn't); New and Rename name a mixtape the same way, in the box where its name is; the bin deletes a file from the browser and from every mixtape. Each side shows its running time.
- **A note kept on screen:** Keep, in a note's right-click panel, keeps that note at the bottom of the screen on every board, as a slim strip of its own paper: its title and about six lines, scrolling inside, so a long note never covers the desk. Right-click the strip for the usual note tools: every button and shortcut works there (bold, headings, lists, checklists, highlight, the paper colour, the text size...). Writing in the strip is writing in the note itself (the card follows, and the other way round; one Undo takes back a stretch of writing). Its header has Go to (the note, on whichever board it is), a fold to just its title, and × to let it go. One note at a time: keeping another swaps it, and Keep again (or × on the strip) lets it go. Handy for an assignment's questions while you look through your notes on other boards.
- **The player:** the clock is a portable cassette player, over a still, faint grain. A dark glass window has two big reels behind it, dimmed, turning while the timer runs, with the time over it in big digits, and the session strip in the cassette's slot at the bottom. Above the window, printed on the player's body: the side (A or B, and what's playing) on the left and where you are in the cycle on the right. The glass is dark on either desk. Under it, five piano keys in a row, each name printed above its key: **Play/Pause** (one key: it says Play while stopped and Pause while it plays, and stays down while it plays), **Rew** (hold it to wind the time back), **Prev** (the track before), **F.F** (the next track) and the red **Reset** (this part from the start, at once, with no winding). There are no meters or counter on the player: the keys are its bottom edge. Through each part the tape moves from the left reel to the right one, so you can see how far along you are (then it turns over for the next part). A small light beside the side is on in the key colour while it plays, blinks while paused and turns green on breaks. The player stands tall off the desk, with a deep front edge and a seam where the lid meets the body.
- **Colour tells you where you are:** cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on a break, dimmed and blinking when paused, quiet silver when ready.
- **The feel:** each digit rolls down into place as it changes, like an odometer, while the old one rolls out underneath and lingers for a moment like phosphor. The colon glows on every second, so you can feel the clock without watching it. Pressing Play eases the digits from the paused colour to the playing one, as pausing does the other way. In the last ten seconds the roll slows down. At 00:00 the digits hold a moment while a soft, low chime plays, like a bell through a wall (a lighter one ends a break). Breaks don't tick: the digits breathe. Reduced motion keeps all of it still.
- **Sound levels** (Settings): a slider for each kind of sound, each playing a little of its sound as you set it: Master, Music, Clock tick, Chimes, Tape keys, Desk and typing, Buttons. A slider at 0 turns that sound off. 100% is the usual level, up to 150%; Back to the usual levels resets them.
- **The tick:** while you focus and Cassie is the window in front, the clock goes tick, tock, never quite the same twice, and softer through the last minute (Settings, Clock: on or off; how loud is under Sound levels).
- **Tables:** the Table key on the left pins a paper card of rows and columns: the top row names the columns and the first column names the rows. Click a picked table's cell to write in it; Tab and Enter move to the next cell or row (and make a new row at the end), Shift goes back, and the arrows move around. Cells copied from a spreadsheet paste in from the cell you're in, growing the table to fit. Right-click any cell (writing in it or not) for a small menu: a row above or below, a column left or right, sort by that column, or delete its row or column (the top row stays; Shift+right-click gives the browser's own menu, and right-dragging still moves the desk). The same menu adds a total row and duplicates or deletes the table; sorting goes high to low, and again for low to high. At the card's top left, Table, Bars, Line and Pie show the same numbers as a graph: every column with numbers can be a series in the palette's colours, and when there's more than one, chips above the graph pick which: click one to take it off or put it back (Bars and Line show any of them, at least one), or, on a pie, which single column it shares out. The choice is kept with the table. Graphs grow in when you switch to them: bars rise one after another (their numbers counting up with them), a line draws itself and its dots pop in, a pie sweeps round; point at a bar, dot or slice for its value. Drag the corner to make a table wider.
- **Radio:** music without files. The Radio key in the Tapes drawer opens a station finder in place of your music: it starts with a few well-liked lofi, jazz, classical and ambient stations, and the box searches by name or kind (lofi, piano, a city...). ▶ listens right there; ☆ keeps a station in your music, where it sits like a track (LIVE instead of a length), and +A / +B puts it on a side, so focus can play one station and breaks another. While the timer runs it plays live, the line under the clock says Radio and the station's name (with a little on-air dot), and Prev and F.F move along the side as usual. Only stations that stream securely (https) are listed, since browsers block plain-http sound on a secure page. The stations come from [Radio Browser](https://www.radio-browser.info), a free, community-run directory: what you search goes there, and the sound comes from each station. It needs the internet ("no signal" otherwise); most stations don't say which song is on, so it shows the station.
- **Rooms:** the Room key at the top right makes a room (with a code like TAPE-K7Q2-MX4R-9WPD: twelve letters and numbers, too many to guess) or joins a friend's; however the code is typed or pasted, with or without dashes, it's understood. Give yourself a name (and, if you like, a picture: click your round face to pick one; it's cropped to a small square, kept in this browser and sent along when you join), and the friends in the room show up as online, each with their picture or the first letter of their name in their colour. The room panel has two faces: before you're in a room, your name and picture, Make a room, and a box to join one by its code; in a room, the code large to share while it's just you, then your friends once they're here (with / to chat and how to send a card), a calm line of how the connecting is going while you wait (the numbers behind details, and a coral line only when something is in the way), and at the bottom who you appear as (click to change it) and Leave. Block is the small circle at the end of a friend's row (it asks once). Pick a note, photo, table or book (or several, with the strings between them) and Send to shows just above the bar along the bottom, at its left: each friend's name as plain text, or Everyone. The card pops onto their desk in the middle of what they're looking at, landing with the pin (several land one after another), with a small "from" tag (click the tag to take it off). Nothing stays in sync: a sent card is a copy. **Messages:** while a friend is in the room, a box to write in sits at the top right, just under Settings, with the messages right under it, so you write and read in one place (with two or more friends, small chips above it pick who it's to: Everyone, or one of them); press / anywhere to jump into it, Enter sends, Esc leaves it. A message slides in at the top right of their screen with your name and the time, and stays: the newest on top pushing the others down, the last twenty kept, and the column scrolls to read back.  Nothing is saved: leaving the room clears them. Up to 140 characters, one every 1.2 seconds, and a friend's are taken at most every 0.8 seconds. You can send once every 8 seconds, a friend's cards are taken at most every 4 seconds (faster ones are let go), at most 20 cards at once, a message over 5 MB is let go while it's still arriving, and Block in the room panel stops someone's cards for good (Unblock is there too). Cards go straight between browsers (WebRTC, encrypted); public WebTorrent trackers only introduce the browsers to each other, and the room code is the password. The room parts (Trystero, MIT, in js/vendor) load only when you join, so the app stays offline otherwise. Some school or office networks block direct browser connections.
- **Hoppers:** folders that open another board (Hopper in the bar). Pick the board on the folder's tab (or make a new one there, which gets a folder back), and click the folder to go; you arrive by the folder that leads back.
- **Picking and groups:** Shift+drag on the empty desk draws a box that picks what it touches; Shift+click picks or lets go of one thing. What's picked stays picked when you click or drag the empty desk (so its Tied to strip stays too); Esc lets go. Dragging any picked thing moves them all. Group, in the bar along the bottom, ties them so they always move together, and gives them a name: a small tag over the group's top left, ready to type in as you group them (pick the whole group to rename it; point at any card in a named group to see its name); Ungroup there too; Delete removes what's picked; Ctrl+A picks everything on the board.
- **Typing on paper:** each key gives the note a tiny strike and a small sideways knock, like a typewriter's, and each letter settles from light to full ink; the typing shortcuts ease the line into its new style (a title grows in, a bullet or box pops in); the space bar thunks, and the note eases taller as lines come. `--` becomes —, `->` becomes →, quotes curl, what you select takes the ink's colour, and there are no spellcheck squiggles. Ticking a checklist box draws a line through the item, left to right, with a soft tick (a lower one when you take it back); a small count of what's done sits in the note's top corner (green once it's all done), and the note glows warm for a moment when the last box is ticked.
- **Undo:** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or the arrows in the bar) undo and redo any change to the desk: moving, adding, deleting, strings, tables, groups, colours, boards. Writing in a note counts as one step from when you start to when you click away. Removed photos can come back too (they're only cleared from storage the next time Cassie starts).
- **Saving and loading** (Board ▾): save this board as an image (a PNG of everything on it), save a backup of all boards (one JSON file, photos included), or load a backup (its boards are added next to yours, nothing is replaced). New, rename and delete boards are there too.
- **Notes worth reading:** right-click a note (or the kept note) and its whole toolbar opens right by the pointer, with the pen put where you clicked, so the format lands on that line; it stays open for a few changes in a row and closes on a click away or Esc (Shift+right-click gives the browser's own menu). The panel has: Title and Heading, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, a highlighter, inline code, bullet, numbered and check lists (click a box to tick it), quotes, moving a list line in or out, a divider line, today's date and Clear. Below that: the colour chip (the eight paper colours, and under them all 32 colours of Cassie's palette, with the ink picked to stay readable), smaller or bigger text, Fit, the paper (plain, lined or grid), Duplicate and Delete. Drag a note's bottom-right corner to make it bigger (long notes scroll inside). While you write in a note, **Shift+Enter** starts a new note to the right, tied to this one by a red string, and you carry straight on writing in it (on the same paper). New notes step past anything already there and keep clear of the clock, and the view glides over to each new one (at the same zoom). A picture pasted while you write hangs beside the note, tied to it, and you keep writing where you were. **Tied to:** pick a card that has strings and the cards tied to it line up in a strip along the bottom, as small pinned copies of themselves (a note's paper colour and first lines, a photo's picture, a table's columns, a folder's board), in order round the card, clockwise from the top. It pairs with the kept note: the strip sits beside it on its right, the two centred together as a pair, with the same header and the same bottom edge (the strip only as wide as its cards) (on a narrow screen it sits just above it; with no kept note, it takes the middle). Click a copy and the desk glides to it and picks it, and its own neighbours take their place, so you can walk along the strings; double-click one to write in it. Pointing at a copy outlines the card on the desk. ← in its header goes back to the card you came from (again, further back); the row scrolls sideways (the mouse wheel does it too), and – folds it to its header (it stays folded until you open it again). The cursor is a thin line in the ink's colour that glides to each new letter, stays solid while you type and blinks once you stop; the line you're typing stays in view (a long note's paper scrolls up under it, and near the screen's edge the desk glides along). Typing stays light however big the desk is: the desk is saved once you pause (and always before the page closes), and what has to be measured for each key is measured once a frame. Shortcuts while you type, at the start of a line: `# ` title, `## ` heading, `- ` list, `1. ` numbered list, `[] ` checklist, `> ` quote. Keys: Ctrl+B/I/U, Ctrl+Shift+X strikethrough, Ctrl+Shift+H highlight, Ctrl+` code, Ctrl+Alt+1/2 title/heading, Ctrl+Shift+7/8/9 numbered/bullet/checklist, Tab and Shift+Tab to indent a list, Ctrl+Alt+0 clear. Pasting brings plain text. The note you're working on straightens up while it's selected.
- **Books:** the Book key on the left makes a hardcover on the desk: a title, a cover colour from the note colours, a cover picture if you like, and its pages, in order (notes, photos and tables). Seen straight on, it has a thick bottom: the spine's colour under the spine, and a cream strip of pages edged thinly by the cover, a little narrower than the cover at the open side. Click it and it opens in the middle of the screen as a spread, the desk dimmed behind: the contents on the left (every page by its first line, the one you're on marked; click one to go there, drag them to reorder), and the page itself on the right, written in just like a note on the desk, right-click panel and all. ‹ and › (or ← → while you're not writing) turn the pages; + Note, + Photo and + Table add a page just after the one you're on (Shift+Enter in a page does too, and a picture pasted in a page becomes a photo page). The title is renamed by clicking it at the top of the contents. **Decorate the cover…** (in tabs: Stickers, Cover, Title, Spine & edges, Extras; with **Surprise me**, which rolls a good-looking combination at random, and **Copy the look** / **Paste the look**, also in the book's right-click menu, to give a series of books the same look without their titles, pictures or stickers) (in the book's right-click menu, or a click on the little cover in the open book) opens the cover big, with the cover's colour and the title's colour from the whole 32-colour palette (the title can also be Auto, cream on a dark cover and dark ink on a light one), the title's look (Bold, Round, Typewriter, Script or Stamp, a stamped box), the spine (its own colour from the 32, or like the cover, with Stripes, Bands, Dots or Stitched on it, the pattern in the title's colour or any of the 32; the open book's binding takes its colour too), the title in **foil** (gold or holographic, the shine following the pointer across the cover; a sticker can be foil too, from its keys), a short **line under the title** ("Vol. 2", "by Lico"), the **pages' edges** (gilded, rainbow, speckled or a colour), a **strap** in any of the 32 colours (the elastic down the cover, a ribbon tied in a bow, a leather strap with a buckle, or a clasp), **little extras** on the cover (a pen clipped on, a sticky note, a pressed flower, a paperclip, washi tape across the corner, a postage stamp, a pin badge, a little taped-on photo, a ticket, a bookmark tassel hanging out of the bottom, a pressed leaf, a feather, a gift tag), a **wax seal** in any of the 32 colours, pressed with an initial (the title's first letter, or one or two of your own) or a little heart, star, moon or leaf, a **charm** hanging from the top corner on a little chain (a heart, star, key, cat or moon; it swings the other way when you drag the book). There's an **Embossed** title style too, pressed into the cover. Any page can be **flagged** (Flag, at the top of the page, in one of eight colours): a sticky tab pokes out of the closed book's edge, as far down as the page is far through the book, and a click on the tab opens the book right at that page; flagged pages show their flag in the contents too. Inside, the page feels like paper: a soft shadow where it meets the binding, a faint grain, and its number printed at the foot. The decorator also has a cover picture, and a sticker tray in ten packs (the last, Kaomoji, is word stickers; see Stickers anywhere): Cute (a cat with whiskers, an exclamation burst, a question bubble, a rainbow, a star, a heart, a sparkle, a flower, a moon, a cloud, a music note, a paw, a lightning bolt, a smiley, a coffee cup, a mushroom), Weather (sun, umbrella, snowflake, raindrop, snowman, storm), Food (strawberry, onigiri, toast, cherries, donut, ice cream), School (pencil, A+, ruler, paperclip, apple, a Done tick) Nature (leaf, cactus, bee, clover, tulip, butterfly), Animals (bunny, bear, frog, duck, fish, chick, penguin), Cozy (cassette, candle, teacup, books, plant, headphones, letter, gift), Space (planet, rocket, comet, UFO, alien, Earth) and Party (balloon, cake, crown, bow, trophy, party popper). Click one to stick it on, drag it anywhere on the cover, and click a stuck one to turn it, size it, bring it to the front or peel it off (a dozen at most; one Undo takes back a whole decorating). All of it shows on the book on the desk, the open book and Tied to, and goes along in backups and rooms. Drop a note, photo or table on a closed book and it goes in at the end; Take out puts a page back on the desk beside the book; Delete (click twice) takes a page away. Esc, the ×, or a click on the dimmed desk closes it, and it opens again where you left off. Find looks inside books too and opens one at the page; books move, tie, copy, back up and undo like any other card.
- **Stickers anywhere:** the Stickers key on the left opens a tray with the same packs as the book covers: Cute, Weather, Food, School, Nature, and **Kaomoji**, word stickers on a little cream label: faces like (=^･ω･^=), ʕ•ᴥ•ʔ, `¯\_(ツ)_/¯` and (╯°□°)╯︵ ┻━┻, ASCII ones like :) :D <3 ^_^ and :3, a few words (OK!, TODO, wow!), or anything you write yourself in its box (up to 30 characters). Drag a sticker out and let go over a note, photo, table, folder, book or the cassette player, and it sticks right there (or click a sticker, then click where it goes; Esc puts it back). Stickers ride along with their card wherever it moves, and keep their spot as it grows; drag one to move it (it can hang over the edge), and right-click it to turn it, make it smaller or bigger, or peel it off. A dozen per card; Undo takes back sticking, moving and peeling on cards, and they come along in copies, backups and rooms. The cassette's stickers stay with it on every board and workspace. An open book has its own **Stickers** key at the top of the page, so pages can have stickers too (they're the same cards: a page taken out keeps its stickers, and a note with stickers keeps them when it goes in).
- **Everything together:** every card has right-click tools in the same small menu (notes and tables their own; a photo: write a caption, put it in a book; a folder: open or pick its board; a book: open, rename, decorate the cover, take all its pages out), and Make a board, Duplicate and Delete work from any of them; the Right-click for tools hint and touch-and-hold cover them all. Cards come together into books in several ways: **Put in a book** (into a book on this board, or a new one), **Bind** in the bar for several picked cards (they become a book in reading order, and their strings to other cards move onto the book), **Make a book** in a note's panel (the note and the cards tied to it, the note first, its words the title), and photo files dropped on a closed book become its pages. Tied to shows a book as a little cover and a topic in capitals, and a folder with how many cards its board holds; a topic is in capitals in a book's contents too. Find looks at book titles and the boards folders open; books can be sent in a room, pages and cover too. The board manager counts a book as one card, and the front page counts books and notes apart.
- **Topics:** Topic, in a note's right-click panel, makes it a heading for the board: its first line is set in big condensed capitals, sized to fit the card's width, between two printed double rules, and anything after it becomes a small italic subtitle. TOPIC is printed small in its corner, with how many cards are tied to it (TOPIC · 4 TIED). Click Topic again to make it a plain note.
- **A note into a board:** Make a board, in a note's right-click panel, turns that note and every card tied to it (and the ones tied to those, all along the strings) into a board of its own, named after the note's first line: they move there keeping how they sit, a folder to the new board takes the note's place, and the new board gets a folder back. Ctrl+Z undoes it all.
- **The right-click panel** is three short rows: the words (title, heading, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, highlight, code, clear formatting), lists and what to put in (bullets, numbers, checklist, quote, in and out, a line, the date), and the card itself: its colour and paper (each a chip that opens a small list), smaller, bigger and fit, and **More** (Topic, Keep, Make a board, Make a book, Put in a book, Duplicate, Delete).
- **Tidy:** with several things picked, the Tidy key in the bar along the bottom lines them up in a **row**, a **column** or a **grid** (as square as it can be), in the order they already sit, starting where the top-left one is. They glide into place, the strings and the group's name follow, and Ctrl+Z puts them back.
- **The desk:** the whole screen is one big workspace with the clock in the middle. The words can be in the Classic fonts (Barlow Condensed, and Varela Round for your notes) or in **Kodchasan**, round and soft, with Thai letters too (Settings, Look, Font; the cassette player keeps its own condensed letters either way). The desk is white (the default) or dark (Settings, Look), each in one temperature: the dark desk is a cool studio (navy, deep-sea keys, mint for breaks) and the white desk is warm paper (cream, wine-brown ink, copper keys, forest green for breaks); notes, photos and tables keep their own paper either way. Each board remembers where you were on it: switch boards (or go through a folder) and you're back where you left it, at the same zoom. Drag empty space (or the clock's face) to move around (let go mid-swipe and the desk keeps sliding, easing to a stop), or drag with the right button from anywhere, even over notes, without picking anything up, scroll to zoom (it eases smoothly toward where you scrolled, around the mouse), double-click anywhere to pin a note there, click a note once to pick it (to move it, format it or walk its strings) and again, or press Enter, to write in it, paste a copied image straight in with Ctrl+V (it hangs where the mouse is; pasted text becomes a note), and drop photos straight on (they always hang whole, in their own shape, with a caption: everyday shapes as instant film (a square Polaroid, a wide frame for landscape photos, a narrow one for tall phone shots), a long panorama as a print held up by two strips of washi tape, and a very tall picture, like a phone screenshot or a page, as a sheet hanging from a binder clip; drag a picture's bottom-right corner to make it bigger or smaller, and it keeps its shape). Drag from a red pin onto something else to tie a string; click a string to cut it (while you're writing in a note, strings let clicks through to the words). Strings run behind the cards at full strength, so they never cross your words (the pins stay on top, where each one is tied); point at a note (or pick it) and its strings turn a brighter red, so you can follow where they go; the cards tied to the one you've picked get a faint red dashed outline, so you can see which notes go together. Each note, photo and table casts one soft shadow. Tables swing on their pin like notes. A card you pick up floats higher, with a softer, wider shadow. A new note lands: it drops in from a little above, its shadow tightens and it squashes a touch as the pin goes in. A new photo develops, milky and pale at first, the picture coming up over a couple of seconds. A card you're brought to (by Find, or the Tied to strip) gives one small settle so your eye lands on it; going through a folder riffles its pages. Brush the mouse across a note or photo and it rocks a little on its pin; pass over a string and it wobbles like it was plucked.  The Boards key at the top of the left column opens the board manager: one row a board (a dot on the one you're on, and how many cards each holds). Click a row to go there (the desk fades over to it with a flip of paper, like going through a folder); pointing at a row shows its keys: rename it right in the row (or double-click its name, or F2), make a copy (cards, photos, strings, groups), or delete it. The board you're on can't be deleted, so nothing goes by accident: go to another board first. Drag rows to reorder them. The box at the bottom makes a new board (type its name, Enter). Up and Down move through the list, Enter opens, Esc closes; with more than eight boards a search box finds one by name. At the very bottom: Save a backup (of this workspace), and Add boards from a backup, which puts a backup's boards next to yours (to open a backup as a workspace of its own, use Load a backup on the front page). The left column's keys are drawn icons (boards, note, photo, table, hopper, find, undo, redo), and so are Group, Ungroup and Delete in the bottom bar; each is named when you point at it. Photos are kept in this browser (IndexedDB), sharp: up to 2000 px on the long side. A picture already that small is kept exactly as it came, so a PNG screenshot stays crisp and an animated GIF keeps moving; a bigger one is scaled down (a PNG stays a PNG when it can). One sent to a friend goes at up to 1400 px if it's very big. To stay light, a big photo shows a 900 px copy until you zoom in close enough to need the full one, each photo is read from storage once per visit (not every time the desk is drawn), and photos far off the screen wait to load. The desk stays quick when it's crowded: drawing it again keeps every card that hasn't changed (so you also keep writing in a note while the desk updates), strings are kept between drawings and only new ones are made, the desk is always one picture the graphics card slides and scales (so starting or stopping a pan or a zoom doesn't repaint it; only once the camera has rested after a zoom that changed the size noticeably is it painted once more, so words come back sharp), the once-a-second effects (the colon's beat, the digits rolling in) restart without making the browser lay the page out, so they never land as a hitch in the middle of a pan, the paper's grain is made once into a plain picture every card shares (instead of a noise filter worked out again for each card at every new size), the zoom is passed only to the few things that keep their size on screen (a group's name, the cursor), and writing in a note never makes the browser lay the whole desk out more than once a frame (the note's plain words, for Find and the contents, are read once you pause). Undo and redo keep every card that didn't change, and picking many cards at once skips each card's little pop.

| `js/app.js` | The `el()` helper, button sounds, the end-of-session notification, offline support and installing |

The scripts are plain files that share one scope: a name declared at the top of one file can be used in every file
after it. Each file starts with a note on what it does.

## History

Cassie grew out of **Solarist**, a study timer where every session was a trip to a new planet. That version,
with its planets, crew and stories, is kept in git under the tag `space-game` (`git checkout space-game`). Saves carry
over: settings, counts and the desk stay where they were, and the space game's progress is put aside untouched (in this
browser, under `lico-solarist-space`) the first time Cassie opens.
