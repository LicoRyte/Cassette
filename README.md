# Cassette Timer

A focus timer like a vintage tape deck: one big clock in a console frame, made to be satisfying to watch while it runs,
on a desk you can move across for your own notes, photos and drawings. No rewards, no streaks to protect. Just the time
passing nicely.

It works offline: open it once and it saves everything it needs. In Chrome or Edge (computer or Android) use
**Install app** (or the browser menu) to get it as its own app with an icon. On iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen.
Everything is saved in this browser.

## Controls

Press **?** (or the ? key at the top left) for every key and gesture. The main ones: `Space` starts or pauses, `S`
skips (while stopped), holding `R` or the Rewind key winds the time back (seconds at first, then minutes a second, never past the start of the part), `Shift+R` rewinds the whole tape (back to session 1), `D` draws, `T` opens Tapes, `0` goes back to the clock, `1`-`9` glide to a bookmark,
`Shift`+`1`-`9` bookmarks what you picked, `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo, `Ctrl+G` groups, `Delete` removes
what's picked, `Esc` puts down the note you're writing or closes Settings.

## What's in it

- **How it works:** the ? key at the top left opens a card with every key and gesture. The bar along the bottom is for the board: which board and its menu, Note, Photo, Whiteboard, Label, Hopper, Find, Undo and Redo. Tapes, Mini, Full and your bookmarks sit at the top left beside the ? key. All are hollow keys like the timer's.
- **What each session is for:** above the digits there's a label: write what this session is for before you press Play (it shows in the browser tab while you focus, too). At 00:00 a small card asks what you remember, written from memory, which is the surest way to make it stick (Ctrl+Enter keeps it, Esc skips). What you keep collects under **Remembered** at the top left (its count shows on the key): open it to read them back, check one against your notes, pin one to the desk, let one go, or write one now. When you keep one, **Check yourself** opens: what you remembered beside the notes you wrote in that session, so you can see what you missed. Settings, Learning turns the question off.
- **Tapes** (T, or Tapes at the top left): a cassette opens on the desk next to the clock; drag it by its top to put it anywhere (it stays there), and **Save cassette** puts it away (everything in it is kept as you go). It has two panels side by side. On the left, **your music**: every audio file you've added (drop files on it, or + Add), kept in this browser. On the right, the **mixtape**: music on or off and the volume, which mixtape is in the deck (new, rename, delete), and its two sides: Side A plays while you focus, Side B on breaks. Drag tracks from your music onto a side (or use +A / +B), drag them to reorder, between sides, or off. Music plays only while the timer runs, fades in and out, crossfades from one track into the next, and dips while the chime rings. When focus turns into a break or back, the deck flips over with a clunk and the music changes sides; the line under the counter shows the side and what's playing.
- **The deck:** real tape: the reels hold brown tape, and a ribbon of it runs between them over two guide rollers, its sheen moving while the timer runs. Two VU meters by the counter swing with the music, and flick with the tick when nothing plays. **Cassette skins** (Settings, Look): classic amber, mint, coral or clear plastic; the whole console's accent follows.
- **The feel of the machine:** music speeds up from a slow, low pitch when you press Play and wind down when you pause or reach 00:00, like a real deck's motor. Each key sounds like itself: Play latches with a firm click, Pause pops back up, Rewind whirrs. 
- **The desk's light:** notes and photos are lit on the side facing the clock, more the closer they are, in the clock's colour (cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on breaks, dim when paused). Paper has a faint fibre grain and a lifted corner, and the grain and a far layer of dots drift a little as you move the mouse.
- **Views:** **Mini** (or M) opens a mini deck, a small window that stays on top of your other apps with the time, the side, the reels, a progress bar and the keys, so the timer is there while you study in something else (Chrome and Edge). **Full** (or F) leaves only the clock, big, with nothing else on the screen; the bar comes back while the mouse moves and the pointer hides when it's still. F or Esc comes back.
- **End of a tape:** when a cycle ends (and on a full rewind), the cassette ejects from the deck with a clack and flies onto your shelf. The counter's wheels click as they roll up, with a heavier thunk when a digit carries over.
- **Mixtapes:** the cassette's Music tab keeps **your music**, every audio file you add, stored in this browser (with each track's length), and **mixtapes** made from it: named tapes, each with a Side A (focus) and a Side B (breaks). Pick which mixtape is in the deck; make new ones, rename or delete them. Drag tracks to put a side in order, to move them from side to side, from your music onto a side, or off a side (the file stays in your music); +A and +B add a track in one click; double-click a track's name to rename it; the bin deletes a file from the browser and from every mixtape. Each side shows its running time.
- **Tape hiss:** a very faint hiss while you focus, fading in after Play (Settings, Feel).
- **The clock:** a landscape panel like a tape deck's front: the label on top, the side (A or B, and what's playing) on the left of the top line with where you are in the cycle on the right, the digits, the strip between the reels, and the keys beside the counter along the bottom. One centred clock in a console frame with amber corner brackets, over a slowly drifting grain and faint scanlines, with a single line sweeping down the screen once a minute. Above the digits: Ready, Focus or Paused, and which session of the cycle this is. Under them: one strip for the whole cycle (a long segment for each focus session, a short one for each break, a longer one for the long break, filling as you go), then two hollow tape-deck keys, Play/Pause and Rewind (Reset), with the key to press written underneath; Play stays latched down while a session runs, and both play a cassette click. Two little tape reels sit either side of the strip: the left holds what's left of the cycle and the right what's done, they turn while the timer runs, ease to a stop when you pause and spin back on Rewind. Under the keys, today's sessions on a three-wheel tape counter that rolls up when a session ends. At 00:00 the screen's edges darken and lift once, like a breath out.
- **Colour tells you where you are:** cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on a break, dimmed and blinking when paused, quiet silver when ready.
- **The feel:** each digit rolls down into place as it changes, like an odometer, while the old one rolls out underneath and lingers for a moment like phosphor. The colon glows on every second, and the edge of the screen glows green once with it, so you can feel the clock without watching it. Pressing Start switches the digits on like an old screen. In the last ten seconds the roll slows down. At 00:00 the digits hold and glow while a soft, low chime plays, like a bell through a wall (a lighter one ends a break). Breaks don't tick: the digits breathe. Reduced motion keeps all of it still.
- **Sound levels** (Settings): a slider for each kind of sound, each playing a little of its sound as you set it: Master, Music, Clock tick, Chimes, Tape keys, Desk and typing, Buttons, Tape hiss. 100% is the usual level, up to 150%; Back to the usual levels resets them.
- **The tick:** while you focus and Cassette Timer is the window in front, the clock goes tick, tock, never quite the same twice, and softer through the last minute (Settings, Clock: on or off; how loud is under Sound levels).
- **Drawing:** press D (or Draw) and the desk becomes paper. Tools: pen, arrow, line, circle, box, frame and eraser (keys P, A, L, C, B, F, E), seven inks and three thicknesses ([ and ]). Everything looks drawn by hand: lines bow a little, circles don't quite close, an arrow bent mid-drag curves. Hold Shift for straight angles, true circles and squares. A fresh stroke is wet for a moment, a little brighter, then dries to its ink. Strokes drawn close together within a moment of each other make one drawing, and a drawing is a thing on the desk like a note: drag it, tie strings to it, bookmark it, group it. The right mouse button and the wheel still move and zoom the desk. D or Esc puts the pen down.
- **Labels:** light, named areas to keep part of the desk tidy (Label in the bar). They're only a place: moving one doesn't move what's on it. Drag one by its name or an edge, drag its corner to resize, pick a colour when it's selected. The padlock beside its name locks it: a locked label can't be moved, resized or deleted by mistake (dragging it moves the desk instead), but you can still rename it. Click the padlock again to unlock.
- **Hoppers:** folders that open another board (Hopper in the bar). Pick the board on the folder's tab (or make a new one there, which gets a folder back), and click the folder to go; you arrive by the folder that leads back.
- **Picking and groups:** Shift+drag on the empty desk draws a box that picks what it touches; Shift+click picks or lets go of one thing. Dragging any picked thing moves them all. Group (Ctrl+G) ties them so they always move together; Ungroup (Ctrl+Shift+G); Delete removes what's picked; Ctrl+A picks everything on the board.
- **Typing on paper:** each key gives the note a tiny strike, the space bar thunks, a soft bell rings near the right margin once a line, and the note eases taller as lines come. `--` becomes —, `->` becomes →, quotes curl, what you select takes the ink's colour, and there are no spellcheck squiggles.
- **Undo:** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or the arrows in the bar) undo and redo any change to the desk: moving, adding, deleting, strings, drawings, frames, groups, colours, boards. Writing in a note counts as one step from when you start to when you click away. Removed photos can come back too (they're only cleared from storage the next time Cassette Timer starts).
- **Saving and loading** (Board ▾): save this board as an image (a PNG of everything on it), save a backup of all boards (one JSON file, photos included), or load a backup (its boards are added next to yours, nothing is replaced). New, rename and delete boards are there too.
- **Notes worth reading:** click a note and its toolbar sits above it: Title and Heading, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, a highlighter, inline code, bullet, numbered and check lists (click a box to tick it), quotes and Clear. Below that: eight paper colours, smaller or bigger text, and Fit. Drag a note's bottom-right corner to make it bigger (long notes scroll inside). Shortcuts while you type, at the start of a line: `# ` title, `## ` heading, `- ` list, `1. ` numbered list, `[] ` checklist, `> ` quote. Keys: Ctrl+B/I/U, Ctrl+Shift+X strikethrough, Ctrl+Shift+H highlight, Ctrl+` code, Ctrl+Alt+1/2 title/heading, Ctrl+Shift+7/8/9 numbered/bullet/checklist, Tab and Shift+Tab to indent a list, Ctrl+Alt+0 clear. Pasting brings plain text. The note you're working on straightens up while it's selected.
- **The desk while you focus:** leave it alone and everything but the clock dims (Settings, Feel: never, or after 5, 15 or 30 seconds or a minute; gentle, medium or deep); move the mouse and it all comes back. It never dims while the pointer rests on a note or photo, something is picked, or you're writing, so you can read in peace. Notes are typed in Courier with a little ink bleed and a block cursor. Cards hang from their pin, swing while you carry them and settle with a bounce; strings wobble when you move either end and drop into place when you tie them. The desk has its own sounds, all made live: paper when you pick up and put down, a pin, a string going taut, a tear when you cut one, a camera shutter for photos, and a typewriter wherever you type (notes, captions, frame names, the label, what you remember): a key click for each letter, a heavier clack for Backspace and Delete, and a carriage return with a faint bell for Enter. Settings, Feel turns the glow, the dimming and the desk sounds off one by one.
- **The desk:** the whole screen is one big workspace with the clock in the middle. Drag empty space (or the clock's face) to move around, scroll to zoom, double-click anywhere to pin a note there, paste a copied image straight in with Ctrl+V (it hangs where the mouse is; pasted text becomes a note), and drop photos straight on (they hang as instant photos with a caption, in the film that fits them: a square Polaroid, a wide frame for landscape photos, or a narrow one for tall phone shots, so 16:9, 3:2, 4:3, 3:4 and 9:16 all show whole). Drag from a red pin onto something else to tie a string; click a string to cut it. Strings run in front of the paper, tied over the pins, or behind it if you prefer (Settings, Look). Strings rest faint, so the words under them stay readable; point at a note (or pick it) and its strings light up bright red on top, so you can follow where they go; the cards tied to the one you've picked get a faint red dashed outline, so you can see which notes go together. With a note picked, Tab walks through every note tied to it, directly or through others: it follows a string as far as it goes, and at a dead end goes back to the nearest note with a string not yet followed, until it has been everywhere (then it starts over). Where a note has several strings, the shortest goes first. The camera glides there keeping your zoom; Shift+Tab steps back. Brush the mouse across a note or photo and it rocks a little on its pin; pass over a string and it wobbles like it was plucked. **Bookmarks:** click a note or photo and press Shift+1 to 9 to give it a number; 1 to 9 glides the camera there and 0 glides back to the clock (the 0 button shows the time while the clock is out of sight). The bar along the bottom switches, makes, renames and deletes desks. Photos are kept in this browser (IndexedDB), shrunk to 640 px.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The page: loads `style.css` and the scripts in `js/`, in order |
| `style.css` | The look: a dark console in the 32-colour palette by BurakoIRL |
| `js/app.js` | The `el()` helper, button sounds, the end-of-session notification, offline support and installing |
| `js/state.js` | What's saved (settings, counts, the desk) and the timer: start, pause, reset, skip, finish |
| `js/screen.js` | The clock's frame, the top bar, Settings, the keyboard, `draw()` |
| `js/digits.js` | The big rolling digits, and the tick |
| `js/feel.js` | Chimes and desk sounds (made live with Web Audio), the green glow on every second, the desk dimming while you focus |
| `js/notes.js` | Rich notes: the formatting toolbar, typing shortcuts, paper colours, text size, and the cleaner that keeps only the tags a note uses |
| `js/draw.js`, `js/pen.js` | Drawings: the hand-drawn shapes, and draw mode (tools, inks, eraser) |
| `js/desk.js` | The desk: the pannable, zoomable workspace around the clock, with notes, Polaroid photos, drawings, frames, strings, picking and groups, undo, bookmarks and the bottom bar |
| `js/export.js` | Saving a board as an image, backups of all boards, loading a backup |
| `js/clock.js` | Colour by state, the colon's beat, the power-on, the soft landing and the breath out at 00:00 |
| `js/tape.js` | The session strip, the tape-deck keys, the reels and the counter |
| `js/music.js` | The two sides of music (Side A focus, Side B breaks) and the Tapes drawer |
| `js/log.js` | The label (what a session is for), what you remember at 00:00, and the shelf of tapes |
| `js/look.js` | Cassette skins |
| `js/view.js` | The mini deck (a floating window) and full-screen focus |
| `js/help.js` | The ? card |
| `js/start.js` | Picks up a timer that was running when the page closed, and draws the first frame |
| `sw.js` | Offline support. Its `VERSION` (cassette-N) and `APP_VERSION` in `js/app.js` are the same number, raised with every change and shown at the bottom of Settings. Files are always fetched fresh when online, so edits show up on the next reload. Bump `VERSION` and update its file list if you add, rename or remove files |
| `manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/` | App name and icons for installing (the cassette icon is drawn in the palette; new icon files get new names, so installed apps notice the change) |
| `fonts/` | Barlow Condensed and Courier Prime (SIL Open Font License, see the OFL files) |
| `sfx/` | Sounds (see `sfx/CREDITS.txt`) |
| `CREDITS.md` | Who made the palette, fonts and sounds |

The scripts are plain files that share one scope: a name declared at the top of one file can be used in every file
after it. Each file starts with a note on what it does.

## History

Cassette Timer grew out of **Solarist**, a study timer where every session was a trip to a new planet. That version,
with its planets, crew and stories, is kept in git under the tag `space-game` (`git checkout space-game`). Saves carry
over: settings, counts and the desk stay where they were, and the space game's progress is put aside untouched (in this
browser, under `lico-solarist-space`) the first time Cassette Timer opens.
