# Cassie

To The Web!: https://licoryte.github.io/Cassie/

A focus timer like a vintage tape deck: one big clock in a console frame, made to be satisfying to watch while it runs,
on a desk you can move across for your own notes, photos and tables. No rewards, no streaks to protect. Just the time
passing nicely.

It works offline: open it once and it saves everything it needs. In Chrome or Edge (computer or Android) use
**Install app** (or the browser menu) to get it as its own app with an icon. On iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen.
Everything is saved in this browser.

## Controls

Press **?** (or the ? key at the top left, next to Settings) for every key and gesture. The main ones: `Space` starts or pauses, `S`
skips (while stopped), holding `R` or the Rew key winds the time back (seconds at first, then minutes a second, never past the start of the part), the red Reset key starts the part over (pressed again at the start, it rewinds the whole tape, back to session 1), `D` draws, `T` opens Tapes, `0` goes back to the clock, `1`-`9` glide to a bookmark,
`Shift`+`1`-`9` bookmarks what you picked, `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo, `Ctrl+G` groups, `Delete` removes
what's picked, `Esc` puts down the note you're writing or closes Settings.

## What's in it

- **The two top corners:** the top left holds the app and the timer as plain words, no frames: **Settings** and **?**, then **Tapes**, then **Mini** and **Full** side by side (the open one is underlined). The top right is only for being online: the **Room** key (Join a room, Room · waiting, or Room with a mint dot once friends are here), the faces of who's here, the message line, and your own face, which opens the room panel just below. Message bubbles stay under the line at the top right: the last twenty, yours and your friends', each with the time, the newest on top; scroll the column to read back. They're cleared when you leave the room. If the Mini window is open, a friend's message also shows across its top for a few seconds, so you see it while you work in another app. With two or more friends, click a face to write only to them; click it again for everyone. Nothing on the screen fades while you focus: the keys and the desk stay as they are.
- **The front page:** Cassie opens on a front page: the name, and your workspaces. A workspace is a whole desk of its own: its boards, notes, tables, photos and strings (the timer, settings, tapes and room are the same in all of them). **Continue** (or Enter) goes back into the one you were in: its name, how many boards and notes, and when you last saved a backup (in red when it's been over two weeks, or never). **Other workspaces** are listed under it, the last opened first: click one to open it (the page fades and Cassie starts again in it). Pointing at a row shows its keys: rename it right in the row, save a backup of it, or delete it (click twice; the one you're in can't be deleted, so open another first). With more than six, a box finds one by name. **New workspace** starts an empty desk with a name you give it, and **Load a backup** makes a backup file a workspace of its own; neither touches the others. Under them, Save a backup and Rename are for the one you were in. The house key at the top of the left column (above Boards) brings it back; Esc steps back.
- **How it works:** the ? key at the top left opens a card with every key and gesture. A column of icon keys down the left side is for making things: the Front page (your workspaces), the Boards menu, Note, Photo, Table, Hopper, Find, Undo and Redo. The bar along the bottom only shows while something is picked, with Group, Ungroup and Delete for several things. A note's and a table's own tools open on a right-click (while the mouse is over a new note or table, faint "Right-click for tools" words float just above it and follow it, until you right-click that one; on a touch screen, hold a finger on a note or a cell for half a second instead). Tapes, Mini, Full and your bookmarks sit at the top left beside the ? key. All are hollow keys like the timer's. Point at any of these keys (the left column, the bottom bar, the two top corners) and a small card shows at once beside it: the key's name in the key colour, its shortcut as a little key, and a line on what it does (a typing shortcut, like # then space, shows as "or type"; long names wrap inside the card), with a small arrow pointing at the key.
- **Tapes** (T, or Tapes at the top left): the cassette opens as a panel beside the left column, framed like Room (a thin frame with the key colour along the top), with your music and the mixtape side by side (it stays put; the desk moves freely behind it), and **Save cassette** puts it away (everything in it is kept as you go). It has two panels side by side. On the left, **your music**: every audio file you've added (drop files on it, or + Add), kept in this browser. On the right, the **mixtape**: music on or off and the volume, which mixtape is in the deck (new, rename, delete), and its two sides: Side A plays while you focus, Side B on breaks. Drag tracks from your music onto a side (or use +A / +B), drag them to reorder, between sides, or off. Music plays only while the timer runs, fades in and out, crossfades from one track into the next, and dips while the chime rings. When focus turns into a break or back, the deck flips over with a clunk and the music changes sides; the line under the counter shows the side and what's playing.
- **The deck:** real tape: the reels hold brown tape. The accent colour (keys, reels, outlines) is the desk's own, on the light desk and the dark one.
- **The feel of the machine:** music speeds up from a slow, low pitch when you press Play and wind down when you pause or reach 00:00, like a real deck's motor. Each key sounds like itself: Play latches with a firm click, Pause pops back up, Rewind whirrs. 
- **The desk's light:** notes and photos are lit on the side facing the clock, more the closer they are, in the clock's colour (cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on breaks, dim when paused). Paper has a faint fibre grain and a lifted corner, and the grain and a far layer of dots drift a little as you move the mouse.
- **Views:** **Mini** (or M) opens a mini deck, a small window that stays on top of your other apps with the time, the side, the reels, a progress bar and the keys, so the timer is there while you study in something else (Chrome and Edge). **Full** (or F) leaves only the clock, big, with nothing else on the screen; the bar comes back while the mouse moves and the pointer hides when it's still. F or Esc comes back.
- **Mixtapes:** the cassette's Music tab keeps **your music**, every audio file you add, stored in this browser (with each track's length), and **mixtapes** made from it: named tapes, each with a Side A (focus) and a Side B (breaks). Pick which mixtape is in the deck; make new ones, rename or delete them. Drag tracks to put a side in order, to move them from side to side, from your music onto a side, or off a side (the file stays in your music); +A and +B add a track in one click; double-click a track's name to rename it; the bin deletes a file from the browser and from every mixtape. Each side shows its running time.
- **A note kept on screen:** Alt+K (writing in a note, or with it picked), or Keep in a note's right-click panel, keeps that note at the bottom of the screen on every board, as a slim strip of its own paper: its title and about six lines, scrolling inside, so a long note never covers the desk. Right-click the strip for the usual note tools: every button and shortcut works there (bold, headings, lists, checklists, highlight, the paper colour, the text size...). Writing in the strip is writing in the note itself (the card follows, and the other way round; one Undo takes back a stretch of writing). Its header has Go to (the note, on whichever board it is), a fold to just its title, and × to let it go. One note at a time: keeping another swaps it, and Alt+K on the kept one lets it go. Handy for an assignment's questions while you look through your notes on other boards.
- **The player:** the clock is a portable cassette player, over a still, faint grain. A dark glass window has two big reels behind it, dimmed, turning while the timer runs, with the time over it in big digits, and the session strip in the cassette's slot at the bottom. Above the window, printed on the player's body: the side (A or B, and what's playing) on the left and where you are in the cycle on the right. The glass is dark on either desk. Under it, five piano keys in a row, each name printed above its key: **Play/Pause** (one key: it says Play while stopped and Pause while it plays, and stays down while it plays), **Rew** (hold it to wind the time back), **Prev** (the track before, or `Shift+N`), **F.F** (the next track, or `N`) and the red **Reset** (this part from the start, at once, with no winding). There are no meters or counter on the player: the keys are its bottom edge. Through each part the tape moves from the left reel to the right one, so you can see how far along you are (then it turns over for the next part). A small light beside the side is on in the key colour while it plays, blinks while paused and turns green on breaks. The player stands tall off the desk, with a deep front edge and a seam where the lid meets the body.
- **Colour tells you where you are:** cream while you focus, warmer in the last minute, mint on a break, dimmed and blinking when paused, quiet silver when ready.
- **The feel:** each digit rolls down into place as it changes, like an odometer, while the old one rolls out underneath and lingers for a moment like phosphor. The colon glows on every second, so you can feel the clock without watching it. Pressing Play eases the digits from the paused colour to the playing one, as pausing does the other way. In the last ten seconds the roll slows down. At 00:00 the digits hold a moment while a soft, low chime plays, like a bell through a wall (a lighter one ends a break). Breaks don't tick: the digits breathe. Reduced motion keeps all of it still.
- **Sound levels** (Settings): a slider for each kind of sound, each playing a little of its sound as you set it: Master, Music, Clock tick, Chimes, Tape keys, Desk and typing, Buttons. A slider at 0 turns that sound off. 100% is the usual level, up to 150%; Back to the usual levels resets them.
- **The tick:** while you focus and Cassie is the window in front, the clock goes tick, tock, never quite the same twice, and softer through the last minute (Settings, Clock: on or off; how loud is under Sound levels).
- **Tables:** the Table key on the left pins a paper card of rows and columns: the top row names the columns and the first column names the rows. Click a picked table's cell to write in it; Tab and Enter move to the next cell or row (and make a new row at the end), Shift goes back, and the arrows move around. Cells copied from a spreadsheet paste in from the cell you're in, growing the table to fit. Right-click any cell (writing in it or not) for a small menu: a row above or below, a column left or right, sort by that column, or delete its row or column (the top row stays; Shift+right-click gives the browser's own menu, and right-dragging still moves the desk). The same menu adds a total row and duplicates or deletes the table; sorting goes high to low, and again for low to high. At the card's top left, Table, Bars, Line and Pie show the same numbers as a graph: every column with numbers can be a series in the palette's colours, and when there's more than one, chips above the graph pick which: click one to take it off or put it back (Bars and Line show any of them, at least one), or, on a pie, which single column it shares out. The choice is kept with the table. Graphs grow in when you switch to them: bars rise one after another (their numbers counting up with them), a line draws itself and its dots pop in, a pie sweeps round; point at a bar, dot or slice for its value. Drag the corner to make a table wider.
- **Radio:** music without files. The Radio key in the Tapes drawer (T) opens a station finder in place of your music: it starts with a few well-liked lofi, jazz, classical and ambient stations, and the box searches by name or kind (lofi, piano, a city...). ▶ listens right there; ☆ keeps a station in your music, where it sits like a track (LIVE instead of a length), and +A / +B puts it on a side, so focus can play one station and breaks another. While the timer runs it plays live, the line under the clock says Radio and the station's name (with a little on-air dot), and Prev and F.F move along the side as usual. Only stations that stream securely (https) are listed, since browsers block plain-http sound on a secure page. The stations come from [Radio Browser](https://www.radio-browser.info), a free, community-run directory: what you search goes there, and the sound comes from each station. It needs the internet ("no signal" otherwise); most stations don't say which song is on, so it shows the station.
- **Rooms:** the Room key at the top right makes a room (with a code like TAPE-K7Q2-MX4R-9WPD: twelve letters and numbers, too many to guess) or joins a friend's; however the code is typed or pasted, with or without dashes, it's understood. Give yourself a name (and, if you like, a picture: click your round face to pick one; it's cropped to a small square, kept in this browser and sent along when you join), and the friends in the room show up as online, each with their picture or the first letter of their name in their colour. The room panel has two faces: before you're in a room, your name and picture, Make a room, and a box to join one by its code; in a room, the code large to share while it's just you, then your friends once they're here (with / to chat and how to send a card), a calm line of how the connecting is going while you wait (the numbers behind details, and a coral line only when something is in the way), and at the bottom who you appear as (click to change it) and Leave. Block is the small circle at the end of a friend's row (it asks once). Pick a note, photo, table or label (or several, with the strings between them) and Send to shows just above the bar along the bottom, at its left: each friend's name as plain text, or Everyone. The card pops onto their desk in the middle of what they're looking at, landing with the pin (several land one after another), with a small "from" tag (click the tag to take it off). Nothing stays in sync: a sent card is a copy. **Messages:** while a friend is in the room, a box to write in sits at the top right, just under Settings, with the messages right under it, so you write and read in one place (with two or more friends, small chips above it pick who it's to: Everyone, or one of them); press / anywhere to jump into it, Enter sends, Esc leaves it. A message slides in at the top right of their screen with your name and the time, and stays: the newest on top pushing the others down, the last twenty kept, and the column scrolls to read back.  Nothing is saved: leaving the room clears them. Up to 140 characters, one every 1.2 seconds, and a friend's are taken at most every 0.8 seconds. You can send once every 8 seconds, a friend's cards are taken at most every 4 seconds (faster ones are let go), at most 20 cards at once, a message over 5 MB is let go while it's still arriving, and Block in the room panel stops someone's cards for good (Unblock is there too). Cards go straight between browsers (WebRTC, encrypted); public WebTorrent trackers only introduce the browsers to each other, and the room code is the password. The room parts (Trystero, MIT, in js/vendor) load only when you join, so the app stays offline otherwise. Some school or office networks block direct browser connections.
- **Hoppers:** folders that open another board (Hopper in the bar). Pick the board on the folder's tab (or make a new one there, which gets a folder back), and click the folder to go; you arrive by the folder that leads back.
- **Picking and groups:** Shift+drag on the empty desk draws a box that picks what it touches; Shift+click picks or lets go of one thing. Dragging any picked thing moves them all. Group (Ctrl+G) ties them so they always move together, and gives them a name: a small tag over the group's top left, ready to type in as you group them (pick the whole group to rename it; point at any card in a named group to see its name); Ungroup (Ctrl+Shift+G); Delete removes what's picked; Ctrl+A picks everything on the board.
- **Typing on paper:** each key gives the note a tiny strike, the space bar thunks, a soft bell rings near the right margin once a line, and the note eases taller as lines come. `--` becomes —, `->` becomes →, quotes curl, what you select takes the ink's colour, and there are no spellcheck squiggles.
- **Undo:** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or the arrows in the bar) undo and redo any change to the desk: moving, adding, deleting, strings, tables, groups, colours, boards. Writing in a note counts as one step from when you start to when you click away. Removed photos can come back too (they're only cleared from storage the next time Cassie starts).
- **Saving and loading** (Board ▾): save this board as an image (a PNG of everything on it), save a backup of all boards (one JSON file, photos included), or load a backup (its boards are added next to yours, nothing is replaced). New, rename and delete boards are there too.
- **Notes worth reading:** right-click a note (or the kept note) and its whole toolbar opens right by the pointer, with the pen put where you clicked, so the format lands on that line; it stays open for a few changes in a row and closes on a click away or Esc (Shift+right-click gives the browser's own menu). The panel has: Title and Heading, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, a highlighter, inline code, bullet, numbered and check lists (click a box to tick it), quotes, moving a list line in or out, a divider line, today's date and Clear. Below that: eight paper colours, smaller or bigger text, Fit, the paper (plain, lined or grid), Duplicate and Delete. Drag a note's bottom-right corner to make it bigger (long notes scroll inside). Keys while you write in a note (Alt+W/A/S/D, Alt+P, Alt+Backspace and Alt+Q work just the same with a card picked and not written in: a new note opens for writing, a photo leaves the card picked, and going back or stepping through picks the card): **Shift+Enter** (or **Alt+D**) starts a new note to the right, tied to this one by a red string, and you carry straight on writing in it (on the same paper); **Alt+W**, **Alt+A** and **Alt+S** do the same above, to the left and below. New notes step past anything already there and keep clear of the clock, and the view glides over to each new one (at the same zoom). **Alt+P** hangs a photo beside the note, tied to it, and a picture pasted while you write is tied the same way; either way you keep writing where you were. **Alt+Left** and **Alt+Right** move the pen a word at a time (with Shift, selecting as it goes). **Alt+Backspace** takes you back to the note you came from, with the pen where you left it and the view gliding there; press it again to go further back, like a browser's Back. **Alt+Q**: hold Alt and tap Q to step through the cards tied to this note (clockwise from the right, the lit one marked 1 / 3, 2 / 3..., the view gliding to each in turn); let go of Alt and you're writing in it (Esc while Alt is held glides back to the note you were in). (Alt+Tab belongs to Windows, so stepping is Alt+Q, just beside it.) The cursor is a thin line in the ink's colour that glides to each new letter, stays solid while you type and blinks once you stop; the line you're typing stays in view (a long note's paper scrolls up under it, and near the screen's edge the desk glides along). Typing stays light however big the desk is: the desk is saved once you pause (and always before the page closes), and what has to be measured for each key is measured once a frame. Shortcuts while you type, at the start of a line: `# ` title, `## ` heading, `- ` list, `1. ` numbered list, `[] ` checklist, `> ` quote. Keys: Ctrl+B/I/U, Ctrl+Shift+X strikethrough, Ctrl+Shift+H highlight, Ctrl+` code, Ctrl+Alt+1/2 title/heading, Ctrl+Shift+7/8/9 numbered/bullet/checklist, Tab and Shift+Tab to indent a list, Ctrl+Alt+0 clear. Pasting brings plain text. The note you're working on straightens up while it's selected.
- **The desk:** the whole screen is one big workspace with the clock in the middle. The words can be in the Classic fonts (Barlow Condensed, and Varela Round for your notes) or in **Kodchasan**, round and soft, with Thai letters too (Settings, Look, Font; the cassette player keeps its own condensed letters either way). The desk is white (the default) or dark (Settings, Look), each in one temperature: the dark desk is a cool studio (navy, deep-sea keys, mint for breaks) and the white desk is warm paper (cream, wine-brown ink, copper keys, forest green for breaks); notes, photos and tables keep their own paper either way. Each board remembers where you were on it: switch boards (or go through a folder) and you're back where you left it, at the same zoom. Drag empty space (or the clock's face) to move around (let go mid-swipe and the desk keeps sliding, easing to a stop), or drag with the right button from anywhere, even over notes, without picking anything up, scroll to zoom (it eases smoothly toward where you scrolled, around the mouse), double-click anywhere to pin a note there, click a note once to pick it (to move it, format it or walk its strings) and again, or press Enter, to write in it, paste a copied image straight in with Ctrl+V (it hangs where the mouse is; pasted text becomes a note), and drop photos straight on (they always hang whole, in their own shape, with a caption: everyday shapes as instant film (a square Polaroid, a wide frame for landscape photos, a narrow one for tall phone shots), a long panorama as a print held up by two strips of washi tape, and a very tall picture, like a phone screenshot or a page, as a sheet hanging from a binder clip; drag a picture's bottom-right corner to make it bigger or smaller, and it keeps its shape). Drag from a red pin onto something else to tie a string; click a string to cut it (while you're writing in a note, strings let clicks through to the words). Strings run behind the cards at full strength, so they never cross your words (the pins stay on top, where each one is tied); point at a note (or pick it) and its strings turn a brighter red, so you can follow where they go; the cards tied to the one you've picked get a faint red dashed outline, so you can see which notes go together. With a card picked, hold Alt and tap Q to step through the cards tied to it (clockwise from the right), the camera gliding to each; let go of Alt and the lit one is picked (while writing in a note, you're writing in it instead). Each note, photo and table casts one soft shadow. Tables swing on their pin like notes. A card you pick up floats higher, with a softer, wider shadow. A new note lands: it drops in from a little above, its shadow tightens and it squashes a touch as the pin goes in. A new photo develops, milky and pale at first, the picture coming up over a couple of seconds. A card you're brought to (a bookmark, Find, Tab) gives one small settle so your eye lands on it; going through a folder riffles its pages. Brush the mouse across a note or photo and it rocks a little on its pin; pass over a string and it wobbles like it was plucked. **Bookmarks:** click a note or photo and press Shift+1 to 9 to give it a number; 1 to 9 glides the camera there and 0 glides back to the clock (the 0 button shows the time while the clock is out of sight). The Boards key at the top of the left column opens the board manager: one row a board (a dot on the one you're on, and how many cards each holds). Click a row to go there (the desk fades over to it with a flip of paper, like going through a folder); pointing at a row shows its keys: rename it right in the row (or double-click its name, or F2), make a copy (cards, photos, strings, groups), or delete it. The board you're on can't be deleted, so nothing goes by accident: go to another board first. Drag rows to reorder them. The box at the bottom makes a new board (type its name, Enter). Up and Down move through the list, Enter opens, Esc closes; with more than eight boards a search box finds one by name. At the very bottom: Save a backup (of this workspace), and Add boards from a backup, which puts a backup's boards next to yours (to open a backup as a workspace of its own, use Load a backup on the front page). The left column's keys are drawn icons (boards, note, photo, table, hopper, find, undo, redo), and so are Group, Ungroup and Delete in the bottom bar; each is named when you point at it. Photos are kept in this browser (IndexedDB), sharp: up to 2000 px on the long side. A picture already that small is kept exactly as it came, so a PNG screenshot stays crisp and an animated GIF keeps moving; a bigger one is scaled down (a PNG stays a PNG when it can). One sent to a friend goes at up to 1400 px if it's very big. To stay light, a big photo shows a 900 px copy until you zoom in close enough to need the full one, each photo is read from storage once per visit (not every time the desk is drawn), and photos far off the screen wait to load. The desk stays quick when it's crowded: drawing it again keeps every card that hasn't changed (so you also keep writing in a note while the desk updates), strings are kept between drawings and only new ones are made, the desk is always one picture the graphics card slides and scales (so starting or stopping a pan or a zoom doesn't repaint it; only once the camera has rested after a zoom that changed the size noticeably is it painted once more, so words come back sharp), the once-a-second effects (the colon's beat, the digits rolling in) restart without making the browser lay the page out, so they never land as a hitch in the middle of a pan, the paper's grain is made once into a plain picture every card shares (instead of a noise filter worked out again for each card at every new size), the zoom is passed only to the few things that keep their size on screen (a label's name and edges, the cursor), and the clock's light on the cards is worked out from their own numbers, at most once a frame, and not while strings sway.

## History

Cassie grew out of **Solarist**, a study timer where every session was a trip to a new planet. That version,
with its planets, crew and stories, is kept in git under the tag `space-game` (`git checkout space-game`). Saves carry
over: settings, counts and the desk stay where they were, and the space game's progress is put aside untouched (in this
browser, under `lico-solarist-space`) the first time Cassie opens.
