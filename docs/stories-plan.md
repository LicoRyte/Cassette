# Solarist: a long, authored story game

Solarist becomes a book of long stories you study your way through. Each story is a **campaign**: hand-written,
set on its own fixed worlds, told in **seasons** of **episodes**. One study session plays one episode. You pick
which story to play, switch whenever you like, and every story remembers exactly where you left it and what you chose.

The random seasons from the last update stay as **Voyages**: short, endlessly varied stories for between campaigns.

## How it plays

- **One study session = one episode.** The episode plays out when the session ends: the ship arrives, things happen,
  the crew talks. Episodes are written to be read in a minute or two, on the break.
- **The break is for reading and choosing.** Most episodes end on a small choice (careful or bold, help or move on,
  tell the truth or keep quiet). Choices are remembered and come back: in later lines, in who trusts you, in endings.
- **Fixed worlds.** Every campaign world is authored: its name, look, colours and story. You can land on it again
  later from the Atlas, and it's exactly as you left it. The planet on screen is always the world the story is on.
- **Your crew is the cast.** Campaign text uses your crew's names, pronouns and traits for the supporting roles:
  the authored characters are new people you meet; your own crew react to them.
- **Seasons end properly.** A season finale closes a chapter of the story and opens the next. Between seasons you can
  carry on, switch to another story, or take a Voyage.

## The story picker

A new **Stories** page in the top bar (and a "Choose a story" button under the timer when nothing is playing):

- A shelf of story cards: title, a one-line hook, its themes, and your place in it
  ("Season 2 · Episode 4 of 10 · last played yesterday").
- **Continue** or **Start**. Switching is free; each story keeps its own place, choices and worlds.
- Finished stories stay on the shelf with their ending, and can be replayed from the start (with your old choices
  shown faintly, so you can try the other way).
- Every story is on the shelf from the start (unlocking them gently was considered and left out). Voyages and Small Hours are always there; a finished story hands over to Small Hours until you pick the next.

## The stories

### 1. The Meridian (exists; wrapped into seasons)

The story already in the game, unchanged in its words, now told as three seasons of five chapters each. Every
chapter is one fixed world and five episodes (its five parts, with their careful or bold choices); the signal at the
end of a chapter is the travel to the next. Its ending stays its ending. 75 episodes.

- Season 1: chapters 1 to 5 · Season 2: chapters 6 to 10 · Season 3: chapters 11 to 15 and the finale.
- Saves that are part-way through the Meridian continue from the same chapter and part.

### 2. The Windtrap Project

*Themes: the beauty of invention, nature, horror, determination.* 3 seasons, about 30 episodes.

Aridel is a desert world. Sixty years ago an engineering collective built the **windtraps** there: machines the
size of cathedrals that breathe in the dry wind and wring water out of it, to make the desert bloom. Then the
collective went silent, and the windtraps stopped. The crew is hired by the last engineer still on Aridel, **Oda
Vey**, to help her start them again.

- **Season 1: Dry Season.** Landing in the dunes. The first windtrap, half buried: its brass lungs, its singing
  pipes, the beauty of something built by people who loved building. Oda: stubborn, funny, very alone. Restoring the
  first trap one part at a time (careful repairs or bold improvising). The first drops of water in sixty years. Rain,
  for one night. And in the condensation shafts, something the crew can't explain: handprints on the inside of the
  glass, very small, very many.
- **Season 2: The Green Line.** The desert greens around the working traps, much too fast: moss, then reeds, then
  trees that weren't planted. Nature is coming back, and it's coming back strange. The missing engineers' logs, found
  one trap at a time. The traps hum at night in a rhythm like breathing. Doors sealed from the inside. Oda insists on
  going on. The horror is quiet and close: something has been living in the machines, and it was here first.
- **Season 3: The Eye of the Storm.** The truth: the windtraps were never just making water. They were holding back
  the great storm that circles Aridel, and keeping something in the sand asleep. The storm is coming. The last
  season is about determination: keeping the machines running through the night, one trap at a time, with everyone
  you've met. The ending depends on the choices along the way: the machines win, the desert wins, or the third way,
  where invention learns to bend to nature instead of beating it.

Characters: Oda Vey (the last engineer), the Collective in their old logs, a family of nomad water-sellers who
don't trust the traps, and the thing in the shafts, never fully seen.

### 3. Second Solarist

*Themes: dream, self-importance, desperation.* 3 seasons, about 30 episodes.

Every child at the Solarist Force grows up on the legend of the **First Solarist**: the expedition that charted the
edge of known space and never came back. Captain **Aurel Vance**, famous, charming and desperate to matter, is
leading the **Second Solarist**: a convoy of ships to finish what the first one started. He invites your ship to
join. At the same time, your crew starts having the same dream, every night: a white shore under a black sky.

- **Season 1: The Invitation.** The broadcast, the convoy, the speeches. Vance is magnificent and exhausting. The
  convoy's other crews: hopeful, young, in love with the legend. The first shared dream. The first world the convoy
  claims, and the first time Vance names it after himself.
- **Season 2: Names on Maps.** Worlds renamed, logs rewritten so Vance looks braver. A ship falls behind and he won't
  turn back. The dream gets clearer and closer: the shore is real, and it's somewhere ahead. Your choices decide who
  listens to you: Vance, his crews, or nobody.
- **Season 3: The White Shore.** Supplies run out. The convoy breaks apart. Vance is desperate: to be remembered, to
  be right, to not have led everyone out here for nothing. The white shore is real. What the First Solarist found
  there, and what it costs to want to be important. Endings: you save Vance from himself, you save his crews from
  him, or you let the legend win.

Characters: Aurel Vance, his long-suffering first officer **Mira Osei**, the convoy's youngest pilot, and the voice in
the dream.

### 4. The Lantern's Last Crew (mine)

*Themes: inheritance, memory, belonging.* 2 seasons, about 16 episodes. Cozy mystery.

Your ship was somebody's before it was yours. Behind a panel the crew finds a photo of its last crew, and a route
scratched into the underside of a bunk. Following it takes you to the places they loved, the people they knew, and
why they gave the ship up. A gentle story about the things we leave behind for whoever comes next.

- Season 1: The Scratched Route. Season 2: Where They Went.

### 5. Tidebreak (mine)

*Themes: community, change, letting go.* 2 seasons, about 16 episodes.

On the ocean world Tidebreak, a city of boats lashed together drifts with the tides. Once a generation the tides
turn, and the city must decide whether to break apart and scatter or hold together and sail into the unknown. The
crew arrives the season it happens, and gets asked to help, and then to choose.

- Season 1: The City of Boats. Season 2: The Turning.

### 6. Small Hours (mine; between stories)

*Themes: rest, small joys.* Standalone single episodes: a broken kettle, a birthday aboard, a rainy night in orbit.
Played automatically when a story is between seasons and you haven't picked the next one, so a session never ends
on nothing.

### Voyages (the random seasons)

The five procedural seasons stay: endless and always a little different. For when you want something short.
